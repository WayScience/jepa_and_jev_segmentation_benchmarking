JEPA + Jev Nuclei Demo

A small, runnable version of the pipeline we discussed: a JEPA learns image features from unlabeled microscopy, a light head turns them into nucleus masks from a handful of labels, and a Jev-shaped decision layer decides which masks to trust and which to send to a person.

Jev itself is in early access and works on text and structured state, not pixels. Here its role is played by a local calibrated model that answers the same request shape (state in, typed questions, probabilities out), so the plumbing is real and the model call is swappable.

The pipeline

Pixels · JEPA
447 unlabeled images→I-JEPA pretraining (predict masked-region embeddings)→frozen features + 16 labeled images→interior / boundary map → watershed → masks
Decisions · Jev
one structured state per nucleus→typed questions: error type, verdict, "is this one whole nucleus?"→accept review reject

The split matters: the vision model does the pixel work, and the decision layer handles the judgment calls that would otherwise be hand-tuned thresholds or a person looking at every cell. Jev's selling points (typed outputs, calibrated probabilities, ~100 ms calls) fit that second job.

Data

The StarDist subset of the 2018 Data Science Bowl: fluorescence images of stained nuclei from many labs and microscopes, each with an instance mask per nucleus. 447 training images and 50 held-out test images, from 256×256 up to 520×696 px. Sixty training images were set aside as the decision layer's "review history"; few-shot label sets were drawn from the rest. All test numbers below are on the 50 test images (2,460 nuclei).

Stage 1 · JEPA pretraining

A ViT with 8×8 patches (192-dim, 6 layers, ~2.7 M parameters) trained for 1,200 steps (25 min on 2 CPU cores) on random 128-px crops, with no labels. Each step hides four blocks of the image and asks a small predictor to produce what an EMA copy of the encoder sees there.

A first attempt collapsed: loss went to zero while features became identical. Adding a variance/covariance penalty on the encoder output, in the spirit of LeJEPA's regularizer, fixed it.

What the numbers say

JEPA pretraining helps over the same network with random weights at both label budgets ({{JF4}} vs {{RF4}} at 4 labels, {{JF16}} vs {{RF16}} at 16), but the gap is within about one standard deviation. A small U-Net trained from scratch is better than both. That is the expected result for a 25-minute, 2.7 M-parameter JEPA on 447 images: the published gains come from much larger models and datasets.

Stage 3 · The Jev-shaped decision layer

Each predicted nucleus becomes a structured state: size relative to its neighbours, shape, how many distance-transform peaks it has, how confident the segmenter was inside it and along its edge, intensity and contrast. The request asks three typed questions. The stand-in model was trained on {{N_QC_OBJ}} masks from the 60 held-out "review history" images and then scored on {{N_TEST_OBJ}} masks from the test set, {{ERR_RATE}} of which are wrong ({{MIX_MERGED}} merged, {{MIX_FRAG}} fragments, {{MIX_FP}} false positives, {{MIX_PB}} poor boundaries).

Simulated review assumes a perfect reviewer who replaces each wrong mask with the true nuclei it touched. Real reviewers are slower and imperfect, so read the curve as the ordering benefit, not a promised accuracy.

Using it for real

Swapping in Jev

  • Replace LocalStandIn.answer() with a Jev call; build_request() already produces a state plus Choice and Noul questions, the shapes TypeSafe's docs describe. Field names may need adjusting to their SDK.
  • Jev reads text and structured state, so give it the per-nucleus feature record (as here), not raw pixels or embeddings.
  • The stand-in learned from 2,510 labelled examples. Jev would answer from instructions and its own training, so test its calibration on a few hundred of your own reviewed masks before trusting the thresholds.
  • At their stated price and latency, scoring the ~1,800 test masks would be one batch of sub-second calls; check data-handling terms before sending lab or patient data.

Making the JEPA part pull its weight

  • Scale up: a ViT-S/B pretrained for hours on a GPU, on tens of thousands of tiles from your own microscope.
  • Fine-tune the encoder instead of freezing it, and use a decoder with multi-scale skips; 8-px tokens are coarse for small nuclei.
  • Benchmark against DINOv2-style microscopy backbones and Cellpose/StarDist, which are the strong baselines for this exact task.
  • Other public sets to try: the full DSB 2018 release, NuInsSeg (H&E), LIVECell (phase contrast), TissueNet (multiplexed tissue).
